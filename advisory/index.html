Annie’s advisory practice is grounded in the belief that selecting art is as much an act of perception as it is an exercise in knowledge. Having spent years within New York City’s emerging artistic communities, she draws from an extensive network of artists and gallerists. She is adept at reading a client’s sensibility — how they live, what they value, the atmosphere they wish to create, and what they may be searching for before, helping them to articulate their own taste. Rather than selecting work through status or aesthetics alone, she looks for pieces that feel emotionally, psychologically, and spatially specific to the people who will live with them.

Her taste gravitates toward work that expands technical, material, or perceptual possibility, as well as work that tells critical stories with precision. Recurring pillars include technology’s capacity to enable new artistic forms, encounters with color as a mode of meditation, and the transmission of an artist’s presence through an object to its viewer. Underlying these interests is a belief that art can reorganize perception — making unfamiliar ways of seeing, feeling, and relating momentarily available.

Selected Project

Sidony O’Neal at EXPO Chicago, April 09 – 12, 2026

Sidony O’Neal (b. 1988; lives and works in Portland, Oregon) uses methods informed by both abstraction and devotion to think through contemporary systems—examining how objects, emotions, and information circulate, fragment, and reorganise. Working primarily with sculptural forms, O’Neal creates precise configurations that reveal an attention to computation and philosophy while remaining deeply grounded in material presence, social complexity, and physical tension.

Works are constructed through recurrence, accumulation, and subtle variation, producing structures that feel simultaneously controlled and unstable. Patterns emerge only to fracture. Singular instances linger long enough to resist dominant organising principles; forms suggest movement and trace rather than permanence. O’Neal’s sculptures possess a quiet sense of velocity, as if caught mid-render, resisting fixed interpretation or narrative resolution. Identity is not depicted directly but is instead caught in the logic of the work itself—distributed across systems, materials, values, and forms.

Works in View

Annie studied art history and computer science at New York University’s Gallatin School of Individualized Study, where her engagement with visual culture developed alongside a longstanding fascination with technological innovation. While completing her degree, she worked at technology startups building alternative social platforms and artificial-intelligence products. These parallel interests culminated in her 2023 colloquium, Methods by which Art Empowers Technology and Technology Empowers New Forms of Art at the Outbreak of Artificial Intelligence, which examined the reciprocal relationship between artistic practice and technological change.

Her experience in technology sharpened her awareness of the absence of human-made art from many digital products. In response, she founded Postscript Internet, a company dedicated to placing work by emerging artists within websites, applications, brand identities, and other digital environments. Ten artists entrusted the project with their portfolios of unsold work, allowing paintings, drawings, and sculptures to circulate beyond conventional art-world channels. The project generated commissions and sales for participating artists while exploring how human irregularity, material intelligence, and aesthetic ambiguity might become integral to technological systems. Its most speculative ambition imagined the eventual integration of art into neurotechnology interfaces; its more immediate consequence was Annie’s discovery of brand design as a field in which these ideas could be pursued structurally.

She has since worked in operations and special projects at design studios including Family Office, Little Plains, and Asimov Collective, while continuing to advise private collectors and place artworks independently.
